HALL-ARRAY NATURAL-VOLTAGE AND PSD MEASUREMENT REPORT
6 x 6 (36-pad) array
=====================================================

Purpose
-------

This report documents the latest five-minute acquisition and spectral
analysis of the 36 Hall-array outputs. All results use the board's natural,
unsubtracted output voltage. The original timeseries_ambient.py file was not
modified.


Acquisition and voltage scaling
-------------------------------

The latest run was acquired on 2026-09-14. It recorded 10,000 transmitted
frames from 36 pads over 300.026 s. The received Q4 ADC values were converted
to voltage using

    V_out = (received value / 16) x (5.0 V / 4096).

Thus one ADC count corresponds to 1.220703 mV. The capture contained no
host-side moving average, smoothing, filtering, baseline subtraction,
windowing, or Welch/segment averaging. The existing firmware does form each
transmitted frame by averaging 64 ADC scans (super_frame(frame, 64)); this is
device-side processing and remains part of the acquired signal.

The natural voltages in this run range from 2.467880 to 2.518082 V across all
samples and pads. The overall mean is 2.494324 V. Pad time-mean voltages range
from 2.469579 to 2.516478 V. The typical within-pad standard deviation is
approximately 0.497 mV (range: 0.455 to 0.598 mV).

The approximately 2.5 V value was not inserted as a constant and was not
subtracted. It is the measured DC level: it comes from applying the above ADC
conversion to the recorded samples and taking their time mean. The spatial
ensemble average has the same time mean, 2.494324 V.


Time and frequency axes
-----------------------

The frame rate was estimated from the host arrival timestamps because the
frames do not contain device timestamps:

    f_s = (N - 1) / (t_last - t_first) = 33.333628 Hz.

For the FFT, samples are placed on a uniform time axis with spacing 1/f_s.
With N = 10,000 samples, the frequency assigned to FFT bin k is

    f_k = k f_s / N.

Therefore the frequency resolution is 0.003333363 Hz per bin and the highest
resolvable frequency (Nyquist frequency) is 16.666814 Hz. The frequency axis
in the PSD CSV/NPZ files is generated from this expression.


Time-domain and ensemble results
--------------------------------

natural_voltage_all_36_pads_timeseries.png overlays the 36 natural-voltage
traces for the full five-minute record. The traces are stable near 2.5 V, with
pad-to-pad DC offsets and millivolt-scale temporal variation.

The ensemble time series is calculated at every recorded time point as

    V_ensemble(t) = (1/36) sum_i V_i(t).

It is an equal-weight spatial average across pads, not an average across time.
Its standard deviation in this run is 0.041 mV; averaging reduces pad-specific
uncorrelated variation while retaining the common DC level.


PSD method and interpretation
-----------------------------

For each pad, and separately for the ensemble time series, the notebook uses
one FFT of the complete natural-voltage record. The one-sided periodogram is

    PSD(f_k) = |FFT[V_out](f_k)|^2 / (f_s N),

with non-DC, non-Nyquist positive-frequency bins multiplied by two. The unit
is V^2/Hz. It is a power-density measurement, not the instantaneous voltage
at that frequency.

The large DC component is expected. A stable voltage near 2.5 V has frequency
zero: it does not oscillate. Consequently its energy is concentrated in the
0-Hz FFT bin. For this record, the ensemble DC PSD is 1866.48 V^2/Hz. This
value follows directly from the measured mean voltage:

    PSD(0) = N x mean(V_out)^2 / f_s.

The spike is therefore the natural sensor/electronics DC output represented
in one narrow FFT bin; it is not evidence of a 0-Hz noise fault. The remaining
low-frequency bins can contain slow board/supply/ambient changes and finite-
record spectral leakage. The single-FFT periodogram also has inherent
bin-to-bin variance.

The ensemble PSD has a much lower non-DC floor than the individual-pad PSD,
because spatial averaging suppresses variation that is not shared by all pads.
It does not remove a common DC voltage or common-mode low-frequency behavior.

Important display note: the PSD CSV and NPZ files retain every bin, including
0 Hz. To make the much smaller non-DC spectrum visible, the two PNG PSD plots
start at array index 10 (0.0333336 Hz) and do not display the first ten bins.
Thus the numerical files and plots use the same PSD calculation and units, but
the plots intentionally omit the DC/very-low-frequency bins for readability.


Conclusion
----------

The present data set provides unfiltered natural-voltage time series, a
36-pad spatial ensemble average, and one-sided PSDs in V^2/Hz. The prominent
zero-frequency component is the measured approximately 2.5 V DC operating
level. The non-DC PSD region is the appropriate portion for evaluating
voltage variation versus frequency; retaining the DC bin in the numerical
files preserves the complete natural-voltage measurement.
